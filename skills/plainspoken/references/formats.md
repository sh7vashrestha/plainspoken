# Notes by type of text

Read the part for the text at hand. The rules in `SKILL.md` still apply to all of them.

## Email

1. Put the request or the news in the first two lines.
2. One email, one subject. Give dates, amounts and names in full.
3. Say what you need from the reader and by when.
4. Skip `I hope this email finds you well` and `Please do not hesitate to contact me`. A greeting and a name are enough.
5. The subject line says what the email is about: "Draft of chapter 3 for your comments by Friday".

## Cover letter and personal statement

1. Open with the role and the strongest reason you fit it.
2. Give two or three things you did, each with what happened as a result.
3. Use the employer's or programme's own words for what they ask for, and answer each one.
4. Leave out "passionate", "thrilled" and "I believe I would be a great fit". Show the evidence and let the reader decide.
5. Write it in the applicant's voice. Add no achievement the applicant did not give you.

## Essay and article

1. State the claim in the first paragraph.
2. Each paragraph makes one point and gives its evidence or example.
3. Deal with the strongest objection in its own paragraph.
4. End on the last point or its consequence. Do not restate the essay.

## Report and summary

1. Lead with the finding or the decision needed.
2. Give the numbers with their units and where they came from.
3. Separate what you measured from what you think it means.
4. A summary is shorter than its source and adds nothing the source does not say.

## Social post

1. First line is the news or the claim.
2. Write as you would say it to a colleague. No hashtag piles, no emoji rows, no one-sentence-per-line drama.
3. Link to the thing. Say what the reader will find there.

## Slides

1. One point per slide, written as a full short sentence in the title.
2. Few words. What you will say aloud stays in the notes.
3. Numbers sit in a sentence or on a labelled chart.
4. No decoration that carries no meaning.

## README and documentation

1. Say what the thing does in the first sentence and who it is for in the second.
2. Give the shortest working example next.
3. Use the reader's task as the heading: "Install", "Run the tests".
4. Commands go in code blocks, exactly as typed.
